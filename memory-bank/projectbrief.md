# Project brief

Build a modern Tasty Planet-style top-down eating game with 3D assets and a gray goo protagonist. The proof of concept proposes four representative levels: Quark Dust Ladder, Coral Colony Tide Pool, Skatepark Bowl, and Tablecloth of Everything. Final build confirmation is pending. The long-term target is 60 levels, beginning at subatomic scale and progressing through cosmic scales to consuming the fabric of spacetime itself. The proposed sampler includes the spacetime endpoint. Keep meaningful relative sizes while freely using cartoon representations at subatomic scales. Scientific literalism must not obstruct the game. A laboratory sample was the provisional starting environment; the sampler can show later environments.

Use the accepted art references and create the playable game for a 60 fps target at 1920 × 1080. Verify the result in the single final visual/performance pass. The selected art direction is smooth cartoon geometry throughout, with cel shading and outlines. Gameplay should resemble the original Tasty Planet. Everything is a reference for scale and theme diversity within the same scale band, not for gameplay. Background story is outside the present discussion; the lab question concerns level-theme progression. Use Godot 4 for a standalone desktop game, targeting the user's local M1 Max Mac Studio first. The game is primarily for personal play; browser delivery is not important and is outside the proof of concept. Precise performance measurement criteria remain undecided.

The user prefers Blender CLI over MCP and confirmed that Astra's reasoning setting is already correct. Use the existing Blender installation through its bundled executable. No game implementation starts before the requested design interview settles the material decisions.

Play is untimed, without time trials or personal-best features. Defer damage and other difficulty systems. Include interesting movement, physics, and interactions between world objects. Consume puddles locally, section by section, like mopping: remove the part contacted by the goo while the remainder forms a coherent translucent mass. The user's proposed abstraction is connected points forming a continuous liquid surface, not independent visible pellets or whole-puddle disappearance. Target roughly 3–5 minutes per typical level, with a shorter tutorial where appropriate.

The protagonist is a rounded rolling ball of goo with physically responsive deformation and sticky floor contact. It must visibly flatten, stretch, and peel away while crawling/rolling. Its appearance and movement must read as goo; rigid-sphere movement with cosmetic wobble does not satisfy this requirement. Steering responds promptly, with some stopping drift and visible body lag/stretch/settling. The exact simulation technique remains undecided. Imagegen references are loose visual guidance; the actual model and motion are the final judge.

Delegate a catalogue of approximately 240 scene ideas and a proposed selection of 60. The root must not spend its quota on bulk scene ideation. Seed the catalogue from the original Tasty Planet levels, with provenance, rather than abstract scale-themed inventions. Include emergent formations and edible part-to-whole progressions. A level can have zero, one, or two size jumps: zoom out to reveal larger things and retire the smallest detail. Jumps are optional and do not gate all growth. Use this catalogue to select the four-level prototype; the larger campaign remains future scope.

Verification follows the user's explicit 2026-09-04 override: substantially fewer tests, one final visual playthrough, then hand the playable result to the user. Inspect Blender work as needed during asset creation. Do not create the standing unit/integration/E2E test matrix or run repeated visual review passes. Basic launch/error checks remain part of getting the game running; measure the required 1080p/60 fps target during the final pass.

Original levels are inspiration seeds only. Do not recreate their 60-level campaign, enforce one-to-one mappings, or generate a fixed set of reskins for every original level. Use their pacing, playful arrangements, interactions, and growth payoffs to invent fresh scenes. Source references document influences, not required replicas.
