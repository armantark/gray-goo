EXPLORATION RESULTS · DESIGN PROPOSAL

From particles
to spacetime.

This proposed campaign selects 60 distinct scenes, beginning with quark flecks and ending with the last remaining spacetime sheet. The original supplies playful eat-and-grow pacing rather than a level template. The gray goo rolls, sticks, peels, and deforms with responsive steering in smooth cartoon 3D with cel outlines. Play stays untimed, with no player damage, combat, quests, dialogue, or new player abilities. Most scenes use shared moving props, attachments, and occasional local liquid. Scenes without camera jumps remain central. The four-scene sampler tests the widest useful range before any campaign production commitment.

This is a scene proposal. The first Tasty Planet informs pacing, playful arrangements, and growth rewards. These are new scene proposals, not copies of its levels. The source is its official Mac demo, edition 1.4.1; identity with the 2006 release is unverified. New interactions, edible parts, and camera jumps are our proposals. A level can have zero, one, or two jumps. No levels are built yet. Exact scale continuity and balance remain open.

Four proposed prototype scenes

SCENE 001 · subatomic

Quark Dust Ladder

A dark particle field holds loose quark flecks beside rounded proton knots.

Eat
Loose flecks, exposed bound flecks, proton knots, compact nuclei.
Interaction
Slow currents deliver loose flecks. Removing edible surface flecks reduces a knot while growing the goo. Dense nuclei remain conspicuously too large.
Size jumps
Zoom out to reveal compact nuclei; retire individual fleck detail.
Parts to whole
Eat accessible quark flecks, then the remaining proton knot once large enough.
Why selected
Tests the opening size gate, readable attached parts, and deformation against simple particle geometry. The science is openly cartoon fantasy.
Design risk
Particle forms and sizes are declared cartoon fantasy.
SCENE 110 · miniature

Coral Colony Tide Pool

A real shallow tide pool holds attached living polyps on small branching coral colonies.

Eat
Plankton flecks, tiny attached polyps, remaining small colonies, larger colonies.
Interaction
A gentle current carries plankton past branches. The goo eats reachable attached polyps and locally consumes shallow seawater, leaving untouched pool sections connected.
Size jumps
Zoom out to reveal neighboring larger colonies; retire individual plankton detail.
Parts to whole
Eat attached polyps to grow, then consume the remaining colony; detached pieces never assemble structures.
Why selected
Tests attached living polyps growing the goo toward the remaining colony, plus local coherent seawater. This is the highest-priority interaction and readability check.
Design risk
Stylized colony sizes and accessible polyps need an early playtest.

Open design work

  • This is a design proposal, not approval to build 60 levels. Target level duration is roughly 3–5 minutes; no duration, difficulty, or food mass budget has been measured.
  • Godot standalone on the user's Mac at 1080p and 60 fps is an unverified target. Deformation, outlines, transparent liquid, and physics must be profiled together in the sampler.
  • Band order expresses coarse growth and setting progression. Exact carryover size, mass gain, food dimensions, and camera thresholds remain design work; some neighboring scenes may need size resets or adjusted starting food, a campaign rule not yet chosen.
  • Keep movement and object eligibility readable before increasing body lag or stickiness. Terrain undulation, cloth, water edges, and camera transitions must not interrupt direct steering.
  • Attached parts require a shared rule for remaining mass, collider updates, and whole eligibility. Removing one part must never double-count its mass when the remainder is eaten.
  • Local liquid needs a finite spatial representation that preserves untouched connected regions. Cloud and spacetime scenes may reuse local removal bookkeeping but should not pretend to be ordinary puddles.
  • Wormhole transport, deforming spacetime, and the final bite are the least routine scenes. Keep their behavior bounded; do not turn each cosmic scene into a new simulation subsystem.
  • Every scene needs enough accessible food after harmless collisions or missed moving props. No essential food may disappear off-map or require timing, combat, or a specific puzzle solution.
  • The sampler trades a conventional kitchen or street for the skatepark because curved ground directly tests the goo. It does not yet verify dense animals, coupled trains, cloth constraints, or full city rendering.
  • Source IDs identify closest inspiration only. Original XML entity and controller labels are evidence; all selected layouts, interactions, growth chains, and camera behavior are new proposals.
