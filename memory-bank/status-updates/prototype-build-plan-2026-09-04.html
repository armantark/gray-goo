Build a standalone Mac game with responsive, sticky goo and a growth journey from particles to spacetime.

The playable scenes

SUBATOMIC

Quark Dust Ladder

Consume quark parts, proton knots, and nuclei. Use cartoon particle forms and one size jump.

TIDE POOL

Coral Colony

Eat individual polyps, then whole colonies. Consume water locally. Use one size jump.

Skatepark Bowl

Catch loose wheels and boards on curved ground. Grow toward rails and ramp sections. Use no size jump.

SPACETIME

Tablecloth of Everything

Consume galaxy knots and fabric regions, then the final remainder. Use one size jump.

Movement comes first

WASD / arrow keysMove relative to the screen. Body direction does not restrict movement.
Hold the left mouse buttonSteer toward the pointer with the same drift and body response.
Hold the right mouse button and dragRotate the view for comfort. Keep the camera tilt fixed.
Scroll wheelZoom within the current scale view. Invisible walls bound the playfield.

The camera keeps a mostly overhead view and stops its movement near field edges. Camera rotation is optional.

The body must feel like goo. It must roll, flatten, stretch, and peel from the ground. Contact and movement must affect deformation.

The agreed play rules
  • Growth: Each scene starts at a suitable size. Every bite contributes growth. The goal size completes the scene.
  • Milestones: The largest target usually supplies the final growth. Smaller food can remain.
  • Eating: Shape and bulk inform a predictable threshold. Edible objects do not require precise approach angles.
  • Engulfing: Objects shrink into the goo without a movement pause. Suitable thin objects can snap. Large bites stretch the body.
  • Color: Food leaves a local color patch. The patch gradually spreads through the goo and leaves a lasting tint.
  • Composites: Parts and wholes have separate thresholds. A whole-object bite consumes only the parts still present.
  • Liquids: Contact removes local sections. The untouched liquid remains a coherent, translucent surface.
  • World behavior: Objects move, collide, carry food, and form structures where the scene supports these interactions.
  • Guidance: A pointer identifies the nearest edible object. An obstacle stays opaque, with a subdued goo outline visible through it.
  • Completion: The goal-reaching bite uses brief slow motion, strong deformation, and a color pulse. Normal play resumes with Next level available.
  • Scale: Growth continues inside each view. An optional size jump reveals larger objects and removes the smallest detail.
  • Pace: Play is untimed, with no damage. Aim for roughly three to five minutes per scene; duration needs measurement.
Build sequence and proof
  1. Prepare the engine. Set up Godot 4 for macOS. Use Blender through its command-line interface for assets.
  2. Build the shared interaction. Implement movement, contact deformation, the camera, boundaries, growth, and object consumption.
  3. Build the four scenes. Add composite parts, local liquid consumption, scale changes, and scene-specific world behavior.
  4. Complete the presentation. Add smooth cartoon assets, cel shading, outlines, color diffusion, and the winning bite.
  5. Verify the playable result. Do basic launch and error checks during the build. Do one final visual and performance pass.

The final pass must cover all four scenes at 1920 × 1080 on the target Mac. The target is 60 frames per second. Performance is unverified.

Then hand the game to the user for play. Do not add the standing unit, integration, and end-to-end test matrix.

Assumptions for confirmation
  • Codex chooses the physics method and tunes camera limits, deformation, color diffusion, food budgets, and size thresholds.
  • The four selected scenes keep their core ideas. Their object arrangements can change to make the agreed play work.
  • After the final spacetime fabric disappears, an invisible surface preserves movement in the void.
  • The full campaign stays outside this build. Godot setup and the playable implementation start after confirmation.
