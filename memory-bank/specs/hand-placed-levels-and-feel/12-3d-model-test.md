# 12: 3D model test

**What to build:** Opus 5.5 builds one new model that the hand-placed layouts need, through Blender, and sees it after each revision in renders from four angles and at game size. It revises until a critic from a different model family scores it above 8.0 on the fixed rubric in `memory-bank/plans/final-shipping.md`. The model then appears in its level.

**Blocked by:** 08 Spawn points and the first hand-placed level, Tide Pool.

**Status:** claimed by opus-5.5 ticket-12 agent

- [ ] Four-angle renders exist for each revision, and each revision has a short note of what Opus saw and changed.
- [ ] GPT-6 Astra scores the final revision above 8.0, and GLM 5.3 Flash gives a second vote. No Opus critic is used.
- [ ] The export check passes for the new GLB, and a native game screenshot shows the model in place.
- [ ] If three successive revisions do not raise the score, the plateau is reported with no lower rubric.

## Work log

**Chosen model: the skate shoe (`shoe`, Skatepark Bowl).** The shoe on master is three squashed spheres with four lace rods. It lies in the gear piles on the bowl rim, beside the helmet and water bottle, and the player sees it up close in tier 2, "Boards and gear", where it is food. Ticket 08 owns the Tide Pool, and the shoe kind already exists on master, so the level wiring does not change.

**Tooling.** The Blender MCP tools were not available to this agent, so the model is built headlessly. `scripts/blender/render_model.py` builds one model through the same builder, bake, and normalize steps as `scripts/build_assets.py`, renders the four review angles and the game view (70 degree orthographic camera at the model's real size, about 49 px across at view size 21), and with `--export` writes the GLB, updates the manifest, and runs the round-trip export check. `scripts/blender/critique_astra.sh` sends one revision to GPT-6 Astra. Renders live under `builds/model-test/rev-N/` (ignored by Git).

- **Rev 1.** Saw: a readable sneaker. The lofted sole with toe spring, the side stripe, and the crossed laces work. Defects: the toe cap and heel counter edges were jagged, the tongue was a bolted-on box, the collar read as a thick donut on top, and the game view was a dark blob. Changed: panels follow whole loft rings and rows, the tongue became a leaning rounded pad, the collar became thinner and dips over the ankle, and eyelets finish the lace ends.
- **Rev 2.** Saw: the heel-counter seam was still a staircase, because the panel border fell inside one UV island of the 512 px atlas. At game size the shoe was still dark: navy canvas, a near-black opening, and a dark collar. Changed: each panel is split into its own mesh after subdivision, so it gets its own UV island and a crisp seam. The canvas is a brighter denim blue, the collar matches the canvas, the lining is lighter and smaller, the tongue is thinner and has a red label, and the game view uses the in-game ground color.
- **Rev 3.** Saw: clean panel seams from every angle. From above, the collar, tongue, laces, and toe cap read at game size. The review renderer drew outlines three times too thick, because the Freestyle line style multiplies the width by 3. This was fixed in the renderer only. First critique pass.
  - Critique of rev 3: Astra 5.70 (recognition 7, proportion 5, game detail 4, material 5, style 7). The laces become a dark patch at game size. The toe-to-sole joint looks chewed. The side stripe reads as a hose. The shoe reads as a padded boot. GLM 6.20 (7, 6, 4, 7, 7), with the same lace complaint and a malformed heel view.
- **Rev 4.** Changed: the heel and collar are lower, and the collar is thinner. The sole is wider and longer than the upper, with a boxier 28-point ring, so the upper no longer shows through at the toe. The side stripe and the laces are flat printed ribbons, and a dark tongue strip lies under the laces. The review outline draws silhouettes only, like the game's hull outline. Saw: the ribbon stripe sank into the sole at its low end and showed as fragments. The flat lace bars read as velcro straps.
- **Rev 5.** Changed: crossed flat-ribbon laces, and the stripe moved up the side. Saw: the ribbon crossings twisted into a zigzag. The dark tongue strip looked like a strap.
- **Rev 6.** Changed: tube laces in three crossings, and the stripe starts ahead of the heel counter. Saw: the crossings were uneven, and the laces were lost at game size.
- **Rev 7.** Changed: four straight ladder-laced bars that stay separate at game size, and the tongue is a soft pad that follows the instep and lifts out of the ankle opening. The first try tilted the pad against the slope, so its sign was flipped. Saw: a clean canvas skate shoe from all four angles. The top view keeps the collar, the tongue, the lace bars, and the toe cap. The lace ends still show as small pegs in the side view. Second critique pass.
  - Critique of rev 7: Astra 6.50 (8, 6, 5, 6, 7). The laces read as straps. The game view crowds the opening, the tongue, and the laces. The tongue is a rigid flap, the canvas looks rubbery, and the outlines break into fragments. GLM 5.80 (6, 6, 4, 6, 7). It calls the back view broken, because the collar edges zigzag and the tongue floats.
- **Rev 8.** Both critics were judging Blender's glossy EEVEE shading and Freestyle line fragments, and the game shows neither. The review renderer now reproduces the game shading: the toon shader's three light bands on the baked atlas, and a 2.5 px inverted-hull ink that is sized per view. Model changes: three woven lace crossings with flush dark eyelets, and a tongue pad that follows the instep and tucks into the throat. The tongue is now lighter than the near-black opening. The collar is thinner, and the stripe starts ahead of the heel. Saw: at game size, the opening, tongue, lace crosses, toe cap, and cream rim now read as separate shapes. The first render hid the lower strand of each cross under the tongue, so the crossings were raised.
